# Player and NPC character rigs — bodies, outfits, equipment visuals, skeletons, animation clips

## Summary

Every NPC in the game is literally headless, and the player is a naked base body with a T-posed tunic floating across his shoulders — both are confirmed in screenshots and both come from a wrong belief encoded in comments: that the Modular Outfits GLBs are "complete full-body variants on the same 65-bone skeleton". Neither half of that is true. outfit_male_peasant.glb contains exactly four meshes (Arms, Body, Feet, Legs), tops out at y=1.559 against base_male's 1.810, and has no head, no eyes and no eyebrows; and the 213-asset library actually contains FOUR distinct 65-joint humanoid skeletons (base_male alone; base_female + all female outfits; all male outfits + all hair/eyebrows; the two animation libraries), not one. Separately, characterRig.attachOutfit clones each outfit part with SkeletonUtils.clone — which by design builds an independent Skeleton from cloned Bones — and parents it as a sibling of the body, while the AnimationMixer is constructed on the body only, so every outfit piece renders forever in bind pose. Equipment has no visual path at all: setMainHand, setHead, equipMainHandAsset, VISIBLE_SLOTS and equippedAssetId have zero callers anywhere in game/src, tests or tools, no ItemDef carries an asset id, and a full 9-slot Kaldite kit renders identically to naked. Finally the draw-call budget is arithmetically dead for anything that is not an NPC: NAMED_CHARACTER_RESERVE is 64 and boot passes maxUniqueDrawCalls: 64, so the non-NPC ceiling is exactly 0 and no enemy in the world can ever be animated; measured at runtime, 872 entities produce uniqueViews: 4 and 8 of the 12 NPCs are identical frozen statues. The one hypothesis I can refute: all 21 clip names in POSE_CLIPS resolve against the 85 loaded clips, so nothing silently falls back — the animation problem is that poseFor can only ever emit 9 of the 15 poses.

## Findings

### 1. [critical/confirmed] dressedAssetFor() replaces NPC bodies with clothes-only GLBs — every NPC in the game is headless

`game/src/world/regionBuilder.ts:57`

**Root cause.** dressedAssetFor() swaps an NPC's base_male/base_female for outfit_<sex>_<peasant|ranger>, and regionBuilder.ts:406 writes that straight into view.assetId as the whole body. The doc comment above it claims the outfit pack ships "complete full-body variants"; the GLB says otherwise.

**Evidence.** GLB ground truth: outfit_male_peasant.glb meshes = Male_Peasant_Arms(2 prims), Male_Peasant_Body, Male_Peasant_Feet, Male_Peasant_Legs. No Head, no Eyes, no Eyebrows. Highest vertex y=1.559 (tunic collar) vs base_male's body mesh max y=1.810 — 25.1 cm of head missing. outfit_female_peasant is the same 4 meshes with no head. Visually confirmed in runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-npc-crop.png: an NPC with body, arms, hands, legs and boots, and the stone wall visible straight through where the head should be. All 12 NPCs are affected (3x outfit_female_peasant, 2x outfit_male_peasant, 5x outfit_male_ranger, 2x outfit_female_ranger). The 7 ranger NPCs are marginally less broken because Male/Female_Ranger_Head_Hood spans y 1.525-1.865 and covers the void — but the hood is hollow; see runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-ranger-npc.png where you can see through the hood opening.

**Fix.** Stop using the full-outfit GLB as a body. view.assetId must stay base_male/base_female (which carries the head, eyes and eyebrows) and the outfit becomes a LIST of part assets layered on that body: outfit_<sex>_peasant_{chest,legs,boots,gloves} or outfit_<sex>_ranger_{chest,legs,boots,gloves,hood,pauldron}. Those part GLBs are byte-identical geometry to the meshes inside the full outfit (outfit_male_peasant_chest's Male_Peasant_Body has identical vert count 3006 and identical min/max to the copy inside outfit_male_peasant), so nothing is lost. SemanticEntity.view currently has only a single assetId and contracts.ts is frozen, so either add view.outfitAssetIds via a root-approved contracts change, or have entityViews resolve an assetId -> parts table locally (a CHARACTER_PARTS map keyed by the same string dressedAssetFor already returns).

### 2. [critical/confirmed] attachOutfit() gives every outfit piece its own skeleton, so the player's tunic and boots are frozen in T-pose

`game/src/render/characterRig.ts:119`

**Root cause.** attachOutfit clones the part with SkeletonUtils.clone and does this.root.add(piece) — a SIBLING of `body`. SkeletonUtils.clone (node_modules/three/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js:392-428) deep-clones the whole graph including the Armature's 65 Bones, then rebinds each cloned SkinnedMesh to `sourceMesh.skeleton.clone()` with `skeleton.bones` remapped to the CLONED bones. The result is a fully independent skeleton. characterRig.ts:106 then builds the AnimationMixer on `body` only, so nothing ever drives the piece's bones.

**Evidence.** runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-town-player.png (crop of baseline-town_center.png): the peasant tunic renders as a horizontal cylinder lying across the player's shoulders with both sleeve openings pointing straight out sideways — the bind/T-pose of Male_Peasant_Body — while the body's arms hang at its sides. The "dark object floating at the chest" in baseline-bank.png is the near-side sleeve opening seen end-on, not a weapon. The same shot shows one booted foot and one bare foot, because outfit_male_peasant_boots is stuck in bind pose while the animated legs moved out of it. Independently reproduced in the sibling agent's runs/corealm/screenshots/eq-02-full-kaldite-kit-spawn-zoom.png. Player is built at boot.ts:299-300 with body base_male + 3 parts, so this is on screen 100% of the time.

**Fix.** Rebind, do not sibling-attach. After cloning the part: build `const bones = new Map(); body.traverse(o => o.isBone && bones.set(o.name, o))`, then for each SkinnedMesh in the piece do `mesh.bind(new THREE.Skeleton(mesh.skeleton.bones.map(b => bones.get(b.name) ?? b), mesh.skeleton.boneInverses), mesh.bindMatrix)` — keep the PIECE's own boneInverses (they encode its authored bind pose) and take the BODY's Bone objects. Then reparent just the SkinnedMesh onto `body` (same world transform, so the default AttachedBindMode stays correct) and discard the piece's cloned Armature entirely. Discarding matters: today each piece leaves 65 orphan Bones in the graph, 195 dead nodes on the player being matrix-updated every frame. Joint names and joint ORDER are byte-identical across all four rigs (verified: 65 joints, identical name list, root/pelvis/spine_01..03/neck_01/Head/clavicle_*/…/ball_leaf_r), so the name lookup cannot miss.

### 3. [critical/confirmed] No equipment renders on the player, ever — the whole visual-equipment API is dead code

`game/src/render/characterRig.ts:232`

**Root cause.** equipMainHandAsset, setMainHand, setHead, VISIBLE_SLOTS and equippedAssetId have no callers. Nothing in loop.ts, boot.ts, gameApi.ts, the UI or the tests ever asks the rig to show a worn item, and no ItemDef carries an asset id, so `equippedAssetId(stack, lookup)` has no lookup table to be handed.

**Evidence.** `grep -rn "equipMainHandAsset|setMainHand|setHead|VISIBLE_SLOTS|equippedAssetId" game/src tests tools` returns only the definitions inside characterRig.ts itself; the only external references to the rig object are boot.ts:298 (build), :303 (add to scene), :304 (setPosition) and loop.ts:232 (play) / :238 (update). content/equipment.ts defines 57 equippable rows across all 9 slots (9 mainHand, 6 offHand, 6 each head/body/legs/feet/hands/accessory1/accessory2) and gear() at equipment.ts:96 emits no asset field. Confirmed visually: runs/corealm/screenshots/eq-02-full-kaldite-kit-spawn-zoom.png (player wearing a complete tier-10 Kaldite kit) is pixel-equivalent to the naked player in RIG-town-player.png — same bald head, same T-posed tunic, same single boot.

**Fix.** Three pieces. (1) Add an item -> visual map, e.g. `game/src/render/equipmentVisuals.ts`: `Record<ItemId, { asset: string; slot: EquipSlot }>` mapping the 9 mainHand rows onto models/weapon/{sword,axe,pickaxe}.glb, the 6 offHand rows onto shield.glb, and the body/legs/feet/head/hands rows onto the outfit part GLBs (peasant parts for tier 1, ranger parts for tier 5/10, ranger hood for head). (2) Implement the body/legs/feet/hands slots in CharacterRig using the same rebind path as finding #2 — a slot swap is: dispose the current part's SkinnedMesh, load the new one, rebind to the body skeleton, add to `body`. (3) Wire it: subscribe to the equipment-changed event (or diff `api.getPlayer()` equipment in Loop.syncPlayerRig alongside the existing rig.play call at loop.ts:232) and call rig.setSlotAsset(slot, assetId).

### 4. [high/confirmed] Non-NPC characters can never be animated: the reserve equals the whole budget, so the enemy ceiling is exactly 0

`game/src/render/entityViews.ts:1033`

**Root cause.** canAffordUnique computes `ceiling = named ? maxUniqueDrawCalls : maxUniqueDrawCalls - NAMED_CHARACTER_RESERVE`. NAMED_CHARACTER_RESERVE is 64 (entityViews.ts:146) and boot.ts:260 passes maxUniqueDrawCalls: 64, so for any archetype that is not "npc" or "boss" the ceiling is 0 while cost is always >= 2. The branch can never be true at any uniqueDrawCalls value including zero.

**Evidence.** __gameDebug.getEntityViewStats() at spawn and in Coldbrace, both identical: {entities: 872, uniqueViews: 4, riggedViews: 4, uniqueDrawCalls: 58, bakedPoses: 20}. Only 4 rigs exist in a world of 872 entities, and all four are NPCs. The 58 is exactly reproducible from the GLB primitive counts: Ilse outfit_female_peasant 4 prims x2 = 8, Dorn outfit_male_ranger 10 x2 = 20, Harrow outfit_male_peasant 5 x2 = 10, Syb outfit_female_ranger 10 x2 = 20 → 58; Carter Bel would need 10 more (68 > 64) and is refused, as are all 6 NPCs in Rootfall and Highcairn. So 8 of the 12 NPCs and every enemy in the game are instanced CPU-baked statues. Worse, the baked pose is chosen in ensureGroup with the ASSET id as the vary seed (entityViews.ts:997 idleCandidates(assetId, assetId)) and frozen at BAKE_PHASE 0.35, so all of them stand in one identical frame — see the ranger in runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-town-center.png.

**Fix.** Change the reserve from an absolute subtraction to a fraction, e.g. `const ceiling = named ? maxUniqueDrawCalls : Math.max(0, maxUniqueDrawCalls - NAMED_CHARACTER_RESERVE)` is not enough — make NAMED_CHARACTER_RESERVE a ratio of maxUniqueDrawCalls (e.g. 0.6) or drop it to ~24 and raise maxUniqueDrawCalls. Then cut the per-character cost so the budget buys more: after finding #1's fix a peasant NPC is base body (3 meshes) + 4 parts = 7 meshes = 14 draw calls, which is worse, so merge same-material parts at build time (Male_Peasant_Body/Feet/Legs all use MI_Peasant and can be one BufferGeometry with one skin) — that takes a dressed peasant from 5 draws to 2-3. Also note estimatedDrawCalls is already 678 against the stated 400 budget, so this must be paid for, not just raised.

### 5. [high/confirmed] Outfit parts are authored to REPLACE the base body, not cover it — bare skin pokes through the trousers and boots

`game/src/app/boot.ts:300`

**Root cause.** The male outfit parts sit on a different, slimmer skeleton (RIG-B) than base_male (RIG-C), and their meshes are cut at or inside the naked body's surface because the pack expects the outfit to be the body below the neck. Layering them over an intact base body leaves the body's own skin outside the clothes.

**Evidence.** Measured cross-sections in bind space. Thigh band y[0.55,0.90]: base_male body maxAbsX = 0.1952 m, outfit_male_peasant_legs = 0.1898 m — the trousers sit 5.4 mm INSIDE the bare leg. Foot band y[0,0.20]: base_male = 0.1865, outfit_male_peasant_boots = 0.1590 — the bare foot is 27.5 mm wider than the boot. Chest: body z range [-0.1635, 0.1253] vs tunic z [-0.1521, 0.1254] — the tunic's back is 11 mm inside the body's back. The female set is the same story despite sharing a rig with base_female (thigh: body 0.2054 vs trousers 0.1879, 17.5 mm of bare skin outside). Visible as the bare leg overlapping the boot in runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-town-player.png.

**Fix.** Do not render the naked base body under clothes. Cut base_male/base_female down to a HEAD CAP and layer that onto the full outfit. entityViews.ts already ships the exact machinery: clipPartsBelow / clipGeometryBelow at lines 1462 and 1506 (used for tree stumps and crop stubble) — add the mirrored clipGeometryAbove. Measured cut planes: base_male at y >= 1.55 yields 1541 of 7147 verts (21.6%) with a clean neck ring of maxAbsX 0.123 and no arm geometry (at 1.52 the T-posed shoulders still catch, maxAbsX 0.508); base_female at y >= 1.50 yields 1628 of 7244 verts with a neck ring of maxAbsX 0.068. Those seams are covered: the male tunic collar reaches y=1.559 (9 mm overlap) and the female to y=1.518 (18 mm). Keep base_*'s Eyebrows and Eyes meshes with the cap, then add hair_short/hair_buzzed/hair_long/hair_buns on top (all are on the male-outfit rig RIG-B; rebind them to the body skeleton with their own boneInverses per finding #2).

### 6. [high/confirmed] 6 of the 15 authored poses are unreachable, fishing plays a tree-chopping swing, and combat has no animation at all

`game/src/render/characterRig.ts:191`

**Root cause.** poseFor() is the only thing that ever feeds rig.play() (loop.ts:232), and it can only return death, mine, produce, farm, eat, climb, run, walk, idle. It never returns chop, fish, attack_melee, cast, hit or bank. It also accepts `inCombat` and never reads it, and it collapses every ActivityState of kind "gathering" onto "mine" without looking at the skill.

**Evidence.** REFUTES the "silent clip fallback" hypothesis: all 21 distinct clip names in POSE_CLIPS resolve against the 85 clips __gameDebug.listClips() reports as loaded (43 in animation_library_1 + 43 in _2, minus the duplicate A_TPose). getMissingClips() would return []. The real gap is reachability. ActivityState (state/store.ts:21-33) has exactly five kinds — gathering, production, traversing, farming, eating — and mining, woodcutting and fishing are all kind "gathering", so poseFor maps all three to "mine", whose clip is TreeChopping_Loop: the player mimes chopping a tree while fishing. attack_melee (Sword_Attack), cast (Spell_Simple_Shoot), hit (Hit_Chest) and bank (Chest_Open) are wired to real, loaded clips that are never played once in a session.

**Fix.** Widen poseFor's input to carry the gathering skill (ActivityState.skill is already on the state object read at gameApi.ts:116, it is just dropped when flattening to activityKind) and branch: mining -> "mine", woodcutting -> "chop", fishing -> "fish". Read input.inCombat and return "attack_melee" on a swing. Drive "hit" from the combat-hit stream Loop already consumes at loop.ts:718 (setCombatHits) and "bank" from the bank-open interaction. Note also that play() early-returns at characterRig.ts:146 without updating this.current when an action fails to resolve, which would wedge the state machine — set this.current before the guard.

### 7. [medium/confirmed] The asset library has four distinct 65-joint skeletons, not one — the code's core assumption is wrong

`game/src/render/characterRig.ts:4`

**Root cause.** The file header asserts "every character pack and both animation libraries share ONE identical 65-bone skeleton", and entityViews.ts:107 repeats it. They share joint NAMES and ORDER, but not bind poses or inverse bind matrices.

**Evidence.** Hashing each GLB's inverseBindMatrices buffer across models/character, models/outfit and models/animation gives four distinct 65-joint groups: (A) base_female + hair_buns + hair_long + all 11 female outfit parts; (B) eyebrows + hair_beard + hair_buzzed + hair_short + all 11 male outfit parts; (C) base_male, alone; (D) both animation libraries. Rest world positions: base_male hand_r (-0.7065, 1.4555, -0.0654) vs male-outfit rig (-0.6832, …) = 23.3 mm apart; foot_l 0.1143 vs 0.0906 = 23.7 mm. base_female Head sits at y 1.550 vs 1.600 for both male rigs. The animation rig is a third proportion again: hand_r at -0.739 (33 mm past base_male, 56 mm past the outfits) and Head at 1.569. Every clip carries translation tracks for all 65 bones (195 channels = 65 x translation/rotation/scale), so playing any clip OVERWRITES the target's bone offsets with the animation rig's proportions.

**Fix.** Correct the comments, then make the rebind explicit about it: always construct the piece's Skeleton with the BODY's Bone objects and the PIECE's own boneInverses. That is what makes cross-rig mixing correct — the residual is the rest-pose delta, which is 0 mm for base_female + female parts and <= 23.7 mm for base_male + male parts, and it is hidden inside the clothing everywhere except the wrists. Do NOT try to "retarget": the joint names and order match exactly, so SkeletonUtils.retarget is unnecessary and would only add error.

### 8. [medium/confirmed] The weapon socket transform in equipMainHandAsset lands the grip off the back of the fist

`game/src/render/characterRig.ts:241`

**Root cause.** weapon.rotation.set(Math.PI/2, 0, 0) with position (0, 0.03, 0.04) parented to the hand_r Bone. The rotation is actually right; the offset is not, and it is the same constant for four weapons whose origins sit in four different places.

**Evidence.** Measured hand_r local basis in the rest pose (identical structure on base_male and the outfit rigs): fingers run along local +Y (middle_01_r at local (-0.0047, 0.1154, 0.0151), middle_03_r at +0.183), index_01_r at local z +0.0406 and pinky_01_r at z -0.0346 so the knuckle line is the local Z axis, thumb_03_r at local (-0.0639, 0.0929, 0.0696) so the palm normal is local -X, and lowerarm_r at local (0, -0.2435, -0.0076). The grip axis of a closed fist is therefore local Z with the blade exiting +Z (thumb/index side); rotX(+PI/2) maps asset +Y onto local +Z, which is correct. Decoded weapon geometry (all four are KHR_mesh_quantization, normalized int16, with a node-level scale): sword.glb spans asset y [-0.208, +0.924] with the origin at the guard, axe.glb y [-0.381, +0.446], pickaxe.glb y [-0.494, +0.704] with the head spread across x [-0.410, +0.403], shield.glb 0.615 x 0.621 x 0.179 with its face in XY and the boss toward +Z. With position (0, 0.03, 0.04) the sword's grip occupies local z [-0.17, +0.04], i.e. the entire 21 cm grip and pommel hang out past the pinky and only the guard touches the fist. None of this is currently observable because the function is never called (finding #3).

**Fix.** Fist centre in hand_r local space is approximately (-0.010, 0.085, 0.000). Keep rotation.set(Math.PI/2, 0, 0) for sword/axe/pickaxe (asset +Y -> local +Z) and set per-asset offsets so each grip centre lands there: sword.glb grip centre at asset y = -0.10 -> position (-0.010, 0.085, 0.100); axe.glb grip centre at asset y = -0.25 -> position (-0.010, 0.085, 0.250); pickaxe.glb grip centre at asset y = -0.15 -> position (-0.010, 0.085, 0.150), plus an extra PI/2 about the shaft (use a quaternion, not Euler, e.g. q = qX(PI/2) * qY(PI/2)) so the head's asset-X spread points fore-aft instead of straight up. shield.glb belongs on hand_l (mirrored basis: fingers +Y, thumb at +X, index +Z, pinky -Z): map asset +Z (face normal) to local -X and asset +Y to local +Z, offset (0.010, 0.085, 0.000). Store these as a per-asset socket table rather than one shared constant.

### 9. [medium/confirmed] The player has no hair and NPCs lose their eyes and eyebrows entirely

`game/src/app/boot.ts:299`

**Root cause.** boot.ts builds the player from base_male + three clothing parts and nothing else. base_male ships Eyebrows and Eyes as separate skinned meshes so the player has a face, but no hair asset is ever attached. NPCs, being clothes-only per finding #1, have no face meshes at all.

**Evidence.** base_male.glb meshes: Face (MI_Hair_1, y 1.695-1.725 — the eyebrows), Face.001 (MI_Eyes, y 1.684-1.714), Sphere.005_Retopology.004 (MI_Superhero_Male, the body). The player renders bald in runs/corealm/screenshots/RIG-player-crop.png. Six unused head assets exist and all six carry a 65-joint skeleton with the same joint names: hair_short (mesh Hair_SimpleParted, y 1.661-1.840), hair_buzzed, hair_long, hair_buns, hair_beard, eyebrows — all on rig group B.

**Fix.** Once the rebind from finding #2 exists, hair is one more part: pick from [hair_short, hair_buzzed, hair_long, hair_buns] deterministically off the entity id via core/rng.ts (same pattern dressedAssetFor already uses), rebind to the body skeleton with the hair's own boneInverses, and add hair_beard for a subset of male NPCs. They skin to the Head bone so they follow head motion for free. The head cap from finding #5 carries Eyebrows and Eyes with it.

## Recommendations

1. Fix the headless NPCs first — it is the single loudest broken thing on screen. Change regionBuilder.ts:57 dressedAssetFor to return a BODY (base_male / base_female, unchanged from what regions.ts authors) plus a list of outfit PART asset ids, and have entityViews assemble the character from body + parts.
